How Sous Chef works

Sous Chef turns what you do in QGIS into a recipe: a Processing model you can run again on other data. Recipes are saved in the Recipe Book group of the Processing Toolbox.

Making a recipe

  • Record: press the record button on the Sous Chef toolbar, work as usual, then press it again to stop. While recording you can pause (to try things out), add a note to the step you just ran, look at what's been recorded so far, or throw the recording away.
  • From a layer: right-click a layer and choose Create Recipe from This Layer…. Sous Chef traces the steps that made it, back to the layers it started from.
  • Tweak a saved recipe: Processing › Sous Chef › Open a Recipe to Review or Tweak…, or right-click it in the Recipe Book.

What gets recorded

Processing tools (also batch and “iterate” runs), the Field Calculator, Save Features As, selections used by a tool, layer filters, joins set up in Layer Properties, the Raster Calculator window and virtual layers. A recipe you run while recording is unpacked into its own steps. Typed values, digitising and other edits by hand can't be repeated by a recipe: they're listed as warnings.

The review window

  • Ingredients are what the recipe asks for when it runs: the layers to choose (amber) and any settings you choose to ask for (violet). Click a setting in a step and choose “Ask when the recipe runs”.
  • Steps that look like repeats, earlier attempts or discarded results start left out, with the reason; tick them back in if needed.
  • For each result, choose whether it's a final output, an intermediate result that isn't kept, or both.
  • Taste Test runs the recipe on the layers you recorded with and compares the results.
  • Undo (Ctrl+Z) takes back the last change.
  • Images & Animations… makes pictures of the workflow, not the recipe itself: a still image (thirteen designs, from flowchart cards to a radial flowchart, sticky notes and a comic strip), an animation as a GIF or an MP4 video (maps drawn step by step, a metro ride, a knight's pilgrimage, a kitchen, a board game and more) or a written recipe (a fun cookbook card, plain instructions, a methods paragraph for a report, or a checklist). Star your favourite designs to bring them to the front.
  • Share saves the recipe itself as one zip file (with its styles and symbol) or as a Python script.
  • Right-click a step and choose Start the Recipe from This Step's Result to begin from that layer next time (a merged layer, say).

Running and sharing a recipe

Run it from the Recipe Book like any Processing tool. To share it, use Share › Share as One Zip File… (or right-click it in the Recipe Book). Others add the .model3 file with Processing Toolbox › Models › Add Model to Toolbox; they don't need Sous Chef.

Settings and privacy

Sous Chef has a page in Settings › Options. It makes no internet connections. The background tracker keeps notes in memory only, and they're gone when QGIS closes.
